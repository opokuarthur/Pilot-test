A week ago, the greatest goalscorer in football history walked out on his country. He got on a private jet and promised to tell the truth. Last night, he did.

After the World Cup, Ronaldo was ready to quit. He'd even written a goodbye letter. But on the flight home, the federation begged him to stay, and asked who should be the new coach. Ronaldo backed Jorge Jesus. His old coach from Al-Nassr. Jesus came with a plan: Ronaldo plays the first match and the last one. Simple.

But then, Norway. At lunch, the coach asks: can you play 30 minutes? Ronaldo says yes. He warms up from half-time. Five minutes. Twenty. Forty. The final whistle blows. He never steps on the pitch. In his words: "The head coach had failed to keep his word."

Two days later, they sit down and make a deal. At his next press conference, the coach would tell the truth: that this was his plan, and benching Ronaldo after a warm-up was a mistake. They wrote it down. Eight points. Ronaldo approved it. They even hugged. The coach didn't even have to apologize.

Then came the press conference. Ronaldo says the coach did the opposite: aggressive, high and mighty. Jesus told reporters he's the coach, there was no incident, and Ronaldo wouldn't start against Denmark. For Ronaldo, that was promise number two, broken. He arrived at the stadium with the team, and never trained. Reports say the coach and federation president followed him to the airport to stop him. He got on the jet anyway.

But here's the part nobody expected. Ronaldo admits walking out was wrong. He apologized to his teammates, and asked the federation to punish him with no reduction, because he's the captain. And he's not retiring. After any suspension, he says he's ready to play for Portugal again. He's 21 goals away from 1,000.

And now, the timing. He promised the truth "in due course." He picked the exact same day Messi said goodbye to Argentina. Out of every day he could have posted, he chose that one. Coincidence? Or did Ronaldo just steal the spotlight one last time? One legend chose his ending. The other says he's not done yet. So, who's right? And was the timing planned? Tell us in the comments.
