## Narration only (for recording)

You put in 500 cedis. The app shows you've made 2,000. You tap withdraw... and nothing. Pending. Pending. Pending. If this happened to you this year, you're not alone.

YepBit looked like a real crypto exchange. Charts, profits, a community. Then in July, Ghana's SEC warned the public: YepBit is not licensed. Days later, investors started reporting the same thing. Withdrawals frozen.

Then came CWPC. You probably didn't hear about it from an ad. You heard about it from a friend, a family member, someone you trust. That's how it spread. Now reports say thousands of Ghanaians may have lost millions of cedis, and the SEC is bringing in the Cyber Security Authority.

And it's not just two apps. The SEC has named 23 platforms operating in Ghana without a licence.

But here's the thing. Ghana has seen this movie before. In 2018, Menzgold. Thousands of people locked out of their money. The case is still in court today. Before that, in 2015: DKM. Nearly a hundred thousand claims. Over five hundred million cedis. Different names. Same story.

It's called a Ponzi scheme. Your 'profit' isn't profit. It's the money of the next person who joined. It works... until new people stop coming. Then the last people in lose everything.

So before you invest: if the returns are guaranteed and too high, run. If you earn by bringing people in, run. If it's not licensed by the SEC, run. Check first, and the SEC has a free line for that. Because somewhere right now, someone is about to tap 'withdraw.' And it's going to say... pending.
